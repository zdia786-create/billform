Why are my bills going up?

If your bills keep creeping up, you are not imagining it. Here are five real reasons, with the sources, and a 2-minute check to see where you stand.

Facts checked 20 October 2026.

1

The energy price cap rose again

From 1 October 2026 the cap for a typical household went up 4% to £1,723 a year. Ofgem puts it down to pressure on oil and gas supplies because of the Middle East conflict.

Source: Uswitch
2

Gas costs far more than last year, and another rise may follow

Uswitch reports gas costs on the cap are almost 27% higher than the same period last year, and suppliers are already signalling another rise in January.

Source: Uswitch
3

You pay standing charges before you use anything

On the October cap, electricity has a standing charge of 54.83p a day and gas 29.68p a day. Over a year that adds up to roughly £200 and £108, even if you used nothing.

Source: Uswitch (yearly totals are my sums)
4

Broadband and mobile prices rise every year

Which? found the big broadband providers add a fixed £3 to £3.50 a month, and mobile networks £1 to £1.80, at the start of each year of your contract. MoneySavingExpert's 2026 research found almost every one of these pounds-and-pence rises was above inflation.

Source: Which?   Source: MoneySavingExpert
5

Cheaper deals get hit hardest

A flat rise is a bigger share of a small bill. MoneySavingExpert's example: a 3GB mobile deal signed at £10 a month in December 2024 is now £13.60, about 36% more.

Source: MoneySavingExpert

Prices keep moving, so the only way to know is to check what you pay today against a current quote.
